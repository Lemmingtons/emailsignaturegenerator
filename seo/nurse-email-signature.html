Nurse Email Signature Examples and Credentials Order

By Email Signature Generator · Updated 25 October 2026

The short answer: A nurse's email signature should give your name, then credentials in the order the American Nurses Credentialing Center recommends (highest degree, licensure, state designations, national certifications, awards, other), your role and unit, your facility and a work phone. Never put patient information in email, and do not rely on a disclaimer to make email secure.

Nurses email managers, colleagues on other shifts, educators and sometimes patients' families. The signature has two jobs: show your credentials correctly, and tell people the fastest way to reach your unit. Most of the mistakes are in the order of letters after your name.

Nurse email signature examples

What to include

  • Your name followed by credentials in the recommended order, for example Jane Doe, MSN, RN, CCRN.
  • Your role and unit, such as Charge Nurse, Cardiac ICU.
  • Your hospital, clinic or health system name.
  • A work phone or unit extension. Leave out your personal mobile unless your employer asks for it.
  • A short confidentiality notice if your employer requires one.
  • Pronouns, if you want to include them.

What to leave out

  • Do not list every course certificate. Keep national certifications and leave internal training off.
  • Never include patient names, dates or other health details in email, signature or not.
  • Do not use a decorative font for credentials. Letters like RN and CNL must be easy to read.
  • Avoid inspirational quotes. They look out of place in clinical email.

Licence, disclaimer and credential wording on this page is general information, not legal advice. Check the current rules with your regulator, employer or brokerage.

Frequently asked questions

What order should nursing credentials go in an email signature?

The American Nurses Credentialing Center recommends: highest earned degree, then licensure (such as RN or APRN), state designations, national certifications (such as CCRN), awards and honours, and other recognitions. For example: Jane Doe, MSN, RN, CCRN.

Should a nurse include a HIPAA notice in their email signature?

Include one if your employer requires it. A notice does not make email secure or compliant on its own, so never send patient information in ordinary email. The generator can add a short confidentiality notice under your signature.

Can I put my RN licence number in my signature?

You can, and some employers or roles ask for it, but most nurses do not need to. If you add it, the generator's compliance fields place it in small print under your contact details.
