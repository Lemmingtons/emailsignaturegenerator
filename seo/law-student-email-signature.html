Law Student Email Signature Examples

By Email Signature Generator · Updated 10 November 2026

The short answer: A law student email signature should show your full name, J.D. Candidate with your class year, your law school, and your email and phone. Add one journal, moot court or clinic role if it matters to the reader. Do not use Esq., Attorney or anything that suggests you are admitted to practice.

Law students write to professors, recruiters, judges' chambers and practising lawyers, and those readers notice small things. A plain, accurate signature shows your school and class year and makes no claim to a licence you do not have yet.

Law student email signature examples

What to include

  • Your full name.
  • J.D. Candidate, followed by your class year: J.D. Candidate, Class of 2027.
  • Your law school name in full.
  • One role that carries weight with the reader: a law review or journal position, moot court, or a clinic.
  • Your school email address and a mobile number. Recruiters and chambers do call.
  • Your LinkedIn profile when you are applying for jobs.

What to leave out

  • Do not use Esq., Attorney, Lawyer or Counsel. Using them before you are admitted can suggest you are licensed to practise.
  • Leave out your GPA and class rank. Put them on your resume, where employers expect them.
  • Do not add a privilege or confidentiality notice to your school signature. It means little coming from a student.
  • When you work at a firm or clinic, use the format they give you, not your school signature.
  • Do not list every society and competition. Pick one.

Title and degree wording on this page is general guidance. Your university, program or employer may set its own signature format, so check their style guide first.

Frequently asked questions

How do I write J.D. Candidate in an email signature?

Put it under your name with your class year: J.D. Candidate, Class of 2027. Both J.D. and JD are accepted. Use whichever your law school uses, and keep it the same on your resume.

Can a law student use Esq.?

No. Esq. is used in the United States by lawyers who are admitted to practise. Using it as a student can suggest you are licensed, and bar authorities take that seriously.

What should my signature say while I wait for bar results?

Use the title your employer gives you, such as Law Clerk or Associate with a note that you are not yet admitted. Many firms have set wording for this. Do not use Attorney or Esq. until you are sworn in.

Should I list law review in my signature?

Yes, if you hold a position on it and you are writing to employers or academics. Keep it to one line next to your class year, and remove it when the position ends.
