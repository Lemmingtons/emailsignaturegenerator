How to Add an Email Signature in Gmail (2026 Guide)

How to add an email signature in Gmail: step-by-step

  1. Open Gmail in your browser and click the gear icon (⚙️) in the top right corner.
  2. Click "See all settings" from the dropdown menu.
  3. Stay on the General tab and scroll down to the "Signature" section.
  4. Click "Create new". Give your signature a name (e.g., "Work Signature").
  5. Click inside the signature editor. Press Ctrl+A to select all, then Ctrl+V to paste your signature.
  6. Under "Signature defaults", set your signature as the default for New emails and for Replies/forwards.
  7. Scroll to the bottom and click "Save Changes".

Your signature will now appear automatically on every email you compose.

How to paste an HTML email signature into Gmail correctly

Gmail's signature editor accepts rich HTML — including formatted text, images, and links — when you paste with Ctrl+V (or Cmd+V on Mac). The key is using a signature that is built with HTML tables and inline styles, which is how Gmail-compatible signatures are structured.

Tip: Do not paste your signature into Gmail using "Paste as plain text" (Ctrl+Shift+V). This will strip all formatting. Always use the regular paste shortcut (Ctrl+V).

If you create your signature using our generator, click the "Copy for Gmail" button. This copies the HTML-formatted version to your clipboard. Pasting this into Gmail preserves all formatting — fonts, colours, photos, and social icons.

How to set your Gmail signature as default

After creating your signature, scroll up slightly in Gmail Settings to find the "Signature defaults" section. You'll see two dropdowns:

  • For new emails use — select your signature here to auto-insert it on every new email you compose.
  • On reply/forward use — select your signature (or "No signature" for cleaner reply threads).

Click "Save Changes" at the bottom. Your signature will now appear automatically.

How to add multiple signatures in Gmail

Gmail supports multiple signatures. To add a second signature, return to Settings → Signature and click "Create new" again. You can name each signature differently (e.g., "Formal", "Quick Reply", "Sales Pitch").

When composing an email, click the signature icon (✏️) at the bottom of the compose window to switch between signatures manually.

Why your Gmail signature might look different than expected

If your Gmail signature looks broken or different from the preview, the most common causes are:

  • Unsupported fonts — Gmail only renders email-safe fonts reliably (Arial, Georgia, Verdana, Trebuchet, Tahoma, Times New Roman). Custom web fonts like Montserrat or Raleway will fall back to a system font.
  • External CSS — Gmail strips <style> blocks. All styling must be inline. Our generator handles this automatically.
  • Blocked images — Gmail blocks external images by default for some recipients. Use hosted image URLs (Imgur, Cloudinary, your website) rather than base64-encoded images.
  • Plain text paste — Pasting with Ctrl+Shift+V strips all HTML. Use regular paste (Ctrl+V).
Best practice: Always send yourself a test email after adding your signature to check how it renders across Gmail's desktop and mobile apps.

Gmail signature size limits

Gmail has a 10,000 character limit for email signatures. Standard HTML signatures are typically 1,000–3,000 characters, well within the limit. If your signature is close to the limit, simplify the HTML or use hosted images instead of embedded data URIs.

Gmail signature on mobile (iPhone and Android)

Gmail's mobile app uses a separate, plain-text signature system that does not support HTML formatting. To configure your mobile signature:

  1. Open the Gmail app on your phone.
  2. Tap the three-line menu (hamburger icon) and scroll to Settings.
  3. Tap your email account, then "Mobile Signature".
  4. Enter your plain-text signature details.

Your desktop HTML signature will appear when recipients receive your email from a desktop client, even if the mobile app shows only plain text while composing.
