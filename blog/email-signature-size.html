Email Signature Size: Dimensions, Logo and Image Sizes

By Email Signature Generator · Published October 2026 · 7 min read

The short answer: keep the whole signature no wider than 600px and about 100 to 150px tall. Show a logo at roughly 120 to 200px wide and no more than 40 to 70px tall, and a headshot at 60 to 100px square. Upload images at twice the size they are shown so they stay sharp on high-resolution screens, keep each file under about 50 KB, and set the width and height in the HTML so Outlook cannot stretch them.

Most signature problems that look like design problems are size problems: a logo that fills the reading pane in Outlook, a headshot that turns into a blurry thumbnail on an iPhone, or a signature so tall that every reply in a thread is mostly signature. The numbers below are the ones that hold up across Gmail, Outlook, Apple Mail and mobile apps.

How wide should an email signature be?

Aim for 300 to 600px. 600px is the long-standing safe width for email because it fits the reading pane in desktop Outlook and Gmail without a horizontal scroll bar. On a phone, a signature built from tables with fixed widths will be scaled down, so a narrower signature (around 320 to 450px) reads better on mobile. If you send most of your email from a phone, design for the narrow end.

How tall should an email signature be?

Keep it to about 100 to 150px, or four to six lines of text. Height matters more than width because a signature repeats in every reply. A 300px tall signature in a ten-message thread means the reader scrolls past 3,000px of signatures. If you need a banner or disclaimer, consider leaving it off replies. Gmail and Outlook both let you choose a different signature, or none, for replies and forwards.

Email signature logo size

Show the logo at 120 to 200px wide and cap the height at about 40 to 70px. A wide wordmark should hit the width limit first; a square mark should hit the height limit first. The common mistake is uploading the logo file straight from a brand pack at 2000px or more and relying on the email client to shrink it. Outlook for Windows ignores CSS sizing on images, so it shows that file at full size.

  • Export a PNG with a transparent or white background. Email clients do not reliably show SVG logos, and Gmail does not show them at all.
  • Crop the empty space around the mark before exporting, or the logo will look smaller than its box.
  • Check the logo on a dark background. A transparent PNG with dark text disappears in dark mode, so a white background can be the safer choice.

Email signature image size for headshots

Show a headshot at 60 to 100px square and upload it at 120 to 200px square. Crop to head and shoulders so the face is still recognisable at that size. A circle or rounded crop is fine in most clients, but Outlook for Windows ignores border-radius and shows a square, so pick a photo that also looks right as a square.

Why upload at twice the size?

Most phones and many laptops have high-resolution screens that draw two or three device pixels for every CSS pixel. A 100px image shown at 100px looks soft on those screens. Upload at 200px, then set width="100" and height="100" in the image tag. The image is shown at 100px and stays sharp. Going beyond 2x adds file weight without a visible gain.

File size limits that actually matter

  • Gmail's 10,000-character signature limit. Google caps Gmail signatures at 10,000 characters. Images pasted as embedded data count against it, which is why hosted images are better than pasted ones.
  • Gmail clips long messages. Gmail hides the end of a message when its HTML is too large and shows a "View entire message" link. A heavy signature in a long thread pushes messages over that line.
  • Attachments instead of images. Some clients turn embedded images into attachments, so every email looks like it has a file attached. Hosting images on a public HTTPS URL avoids this.
  • Keep each image under about 50 KB. It loads fast on mobile data and keeps the total signature light. Compress PNGs, or use a JPG for photos.

How to stop Outlook resizing your signature images

  1. Resize the image file itself to 2x its display size before uploading. Never rely on the client to shrink a large file.
  2. Set width and height as HTML attributes on the image tag, not only in CSS. Outlook for Windows reads the attributes.
  3. Host the image on a public HTTPS URL rather than pasting or attaching it.
  4. Send a test to an Outlook account and a Gmail account, and open it on a phone before you roll it out.

Font size in an email signature

Use 12 to 14px for contact details and 14 to 18px for your name. Smaller than 11px is hard to read on a phone. Use one font family, chosen from email-safe fonts such as Arial, Helvetica, Georgia or Verdana, because a custom web font usually falls back to Times New Roman in Outlook.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best size for an email signature?

Keep the whole signature 300 to 600px wide and about 100 to 150px tall, which is four to six lines of text. Narrower signatures read better on phones.

What size should a logo be in an email signature?

Show it at 120 to 200px wide and no more than 40 to 70px tall. Upload it at twice that size so it stays sharp on high-resolution screens, and keep the file under about 50 KB.

What size should a headshot be in an email signature?

Show a headshot at 60 to 100px square and upload it at 120 to 200px square. Crop to head and shoulders so the face is recognisable at small sizes.

Why is my email signature image huge in Outlook?

Outlook for Windows ignores CSS sizing on images and shows the file at its real size. Resize the file before uploading and set width and height as HTML attributes on the image tag.

What is the Gmail signature size limit?

Gmail limits signatures to 10,000 characters. Pasted images stored inside the signature count toward that limit, so link to hosted images instead.
