How to Add a Logo to Your Email Signature

By Email Signature Generator · Published October 2026 · 7 min read

The short answer: export your logo as a PNG (not SVG), resize it to about 400px wide, and host it at a public HTTPS address. Then insert it by URL in your email client, or build the signature as HTML and paste it in, with the logo shown at 120 to 200px wide and no more than about 40 to 70px tall. Keep your name and contact details as text next to the logo, never inside the image.

A logo is the fastest way to make a signature look like it comes from a business, and the most common thing to go wrong. Logos turn up huge in Outlook, vanish in dark mode, arrive as paperclip attachments, or show as a broken image icon. Almost all of that comes down to three choices: the file type, the file size, and where the image lives.

1. Get the right logo file

  • Use a PNG. Gmail does not display SVG images and Outlook support is patchy. JPG is fine for a logo on a solid background.
  • Resize it to about 2x the display size, so roughly 240 to 400px wide. A 3000px file from a brand pack will be shown at full size in Outlook for Windows.
  • Crop the empty space around the mark.
  • Keep the file under about 50 KB.
  • Decide on the background. A transparent PNG with dark lettering disappears in dark mode, so a white background, or a version made for dark backgrounds, is often safer.

The full set of dimensions for logos, headshots and icons is in our email signature size guide.

2. Host the logo at a public URL

Email clients handle images in one of two ways. A hosted image is loaded from a web address when the email is opened. An embedded image travels inside the email. Embedded images are why some signatures show up as attachments on every message, and they count against Gmail's 10,000-character signature limit. A hosted image avoids both.

The URL must be public and use HTTPS. Links to a private Google Drive or Dropbox file, an intranet, or a page that needs a sign-in will show as a broken image to everyone outside your organisation. Test the address in a private browser window: if the image opens on its own without signing in, it will work.

3. Add the logo in your email client

Gmail

  1. Open Settings, then See all settings, and scroll to Signature.
  2. Create or edit a signature and click the Insert image icon.
  3. Choose Web Address (URL) and paste the logo's HTTPS address. Gmail shows a preview if the link works.
  4. Pick Small or Medium, then save changes at the bottom of the page.

Gmail's size buttons are coarse. For an exact size, build the signature as HTML with the width and height set, then copy and paste it into the signature box. Our Gmail signature guide has the full steps.

Outlook

In new Outlook and Outlook on the web, open Settings, then Accounts, then Signatures, and paste your signature into the editor. In classic Outlook for Windows, open File, Options, Mail, Signatures, and use the picture button or paste. Outlook for Windows ignores CSS sizing, so the image file itself should already be the right size, with width and height set as HTML attributes. See our Outlook signature guide for each version.

Apple Mail and iPhone

On a Mac, open Mail, then Settings, then Signatures, and paste a signature that uses a hosted logo. Untick "Always match my default message font" so your formatting stays. On iPhone the signature editor does not insert images, so paste a signature copied from a page or email that already shows the logo. Our iPhone and Android guide walks through it.

4. Keep the important details as text

Many recipients block remote images by default, and screen readers cannot read text inside a picture. If your name, phone number and website are part of the logo image, they vanish in those cases. Put them in the signature as real text, and give the logo alt text with your company name, so a blocked image still shows something useful.

5. Test before you roll it out

  1. Send a test to a Gmail address and an Outlook address.
  2. Open both on desktop and on a phone.
  3. Switch the phone to dark mode and check the logo is still visible.
  4. Reply to the test message and check the logo does not turn into an attachment.

Frequently asked questions

How do I add a logo to my email signature?

Save the logo as a PNG about 400px wide, host it at a public HTTPS address, then insert it by URL in your email client's signature settings, or paste in an HTML signature that already contains it. Show it at 120 to 200px wide.

Why does my email signature logo show as an attachment?

The image is embedded in the email rather than loaded from a web address. Host the logo at a public HTTPS URL and insert it by address instead of pasting or attaching the file.

What file type should an email signature logo be?

PNG is the safest choice. Gmail does not display SVG images, and JPG does not support transparency. Keep the file under about 50 KB.

Why is my logo blurry in my email signature?

The file is smaller than the size it is shown at, often on a high-resolution phone or laptop screen. Upload the logo at twice its display size and set the display width and height in the HTML.

Can I use my company logo from our website?

Yes. If the logo on your website is a PNG or JPG, its address can often be used directly. The generator can also read your logo from your website and convert it to a PNG for you.
